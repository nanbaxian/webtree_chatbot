# OSSD Core Guide

Source intent: a compact, RAG-friendly summary of Ontario Secondary School Diploma basics and common course-code language for Webtree Academy students.

## What OSSD means

OSSD stands for Ontario Secondary School Diploma. In Ontario, students graduate by combining compulsory credits, optional credits, literacy requirements, online learning credits, and community involvement hours.

## Graduation requirements

For students who started Grade 9 in 2023 or earlier:

- 18 compulsory credits
- 12 optional credits
- the literacy requirement
- at least 2 online learning credits
- 40 hours of community involvement

For students who started Grade 9 in 2024 or later:

- 17 compulsory credits
- 13 optional credits
- the literacy requirement
- at least 2 online learning credits
- 40 hours of community involvement

## Common compulsory-credit structure

The long-standing OSSD framework includes:

- 4 credits in English
- 3 credits in mathematics
- 2 credits in science
- 1 credit in Canadian geography
- 1 credit in Canadian history
- 1 credit in the arts
- 1 credit in health and physical education
- 1 credit in French as a second language
- 0.5 credit in career studies
- 0.5 credit in civics and citizenship

Ontario also uses group requirements and, for newer cohorts, a Grade 9 or 10 technological education requirement instead of the older group structure.

## Course code reading guide

Ontario course codes usually follow this pattern:

- first letter or letters = subject area
- middle digit = grade level, usually 1 to 4
- last letter = course level or stream

Common stream letters:

- `D` academic
- `P` applied
- `U` university preparation
- `C` college preparation
- `M` university/college preparation
- `E` workplace preparation
- `O` open
- `L` locally developed compulsory credit

## How to explain streams to families

- `U` usually means the course prepares for university.
- `C` usually means the course prepares for college.
- `M` usually means a mixed university/college pathway.
- `E` usually means workplace preparation.
- `O` usually means open enrollment.

If a user says something like "grade 11 math" or "grade 11 science", the safest response is usually to ask which stream or strand they mean unless the context already points to a specific code.
